01Overview

Solenne is forty-two homes set behind a single arcade of pale limestone, facing west across Lake Ilse. Every residence takes the evening light, and none of them looks at another.

The building is low, long and quiet: a line drawn between the water and the hills, with walled gardens running down to a private shore and a pier for swimming before breakfast.

The Western Shore

The lake is eleven kilometres long and never more than two wide. Solenne sits where it bends west, so the sun sets straight down the water in summer and the far shore is close enough to wave to.

From the gate

  1. Ilse village & market6 min walk
  2. Ferry to the east shore9 min walk
  3. Golf & rowing club7 min drive
  4. Mainline station18 min drive
  5. Ski lifts, Col d'Aval50 min drive

Reed beds along the shore are protected, so the view stays as it is: herons in the morning, swifts in the evening, no motorboats.

Behind the water, the hills: forty minutes of switchbacks to the first ridge, and walking paths that begin at the garden gate.

03Amenities

Eight shared rooms, each built around one quiet thing: water, steam, books, shade, wine, a boat, citrus, and arriving home.

04Architecture

One continuous arcade, 214 metres long, in hand-dressed limestone from a quarry twelve kilometres up the valley.

05Residences

One- to three-bedroom apartments and four penthouses, all dual aspect, all with a loggia on the water. Ceilings are 3.2 metres; the windows go all the way down.

Specification

Load-bearing limestone over a concrete frame. Triple-glazed timber windows, full height, with external linen blinds set into every arch.

Lime plaster walls, wide-board oak floors, honed stone in kitchens and bathrooms. Joinery in oiled oak, made in the valley.

Lake-source heat pumps for heating and cooling, photovoltaic roofs on the pavilions, and heat recovery ventilation in every home.

A resident manager on site, mooring for every home, secure underground parking with charging, and a daily boat to the village in summer.
